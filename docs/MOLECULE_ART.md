# Molecular specimen assets

Generated with the built-in image_gen tool. Production asset: `assets/art/molecular-fragments.png` (transparent 4 × 4 atlas). The original image is retained in the Codex generated-images directory; the game only loads the repository copy.

The first twelve cells follow the fragment IDs in `data/molecule.json`; the last four are core, carbon, hydrogen and chain. Graphite carbon, silver bonds and sparse element colors replace the earlier rainbow fragment spheres. These are illustrative game motifs, not chemically validated atom/valence models.

`MoleculeSpecimen` caches alpha-bounded atlas regions and scales each chemical group separately. `MoleculeView` draws live bonds, fragment placement, selection and reaction light. The editor, hub, lineage thumbnails and battle share the same assets. No face or instrument disk is drawn at the center. Hit motion and property/weakness flashes still follow actual site IDs.

## Production prompt

Create a production transparent sprite ATLAS for a dark scientific drug-discovery game, one square sheet with EXACTLY 4 columns by 4 rows, equal cells, no grid lines. Each object centered in its cell with generous transparent gutters (20% margin), fully isolated, no overlaps between cells. Orthographic three-quarter view from slightly above, consistent orientation and atom scale, studio key light upper left, cool cyan rim light right, physically rendered graphite carbon atoms with silver specular reflections, slim polished titanium bonds, sparse dark crimson oxygen, desaturated teal nitrogen, pearl hydrogen. Detailed realistic scientific ball-and-stick molecular fragments, NOT toy plastic, NOT oversized rainbow spheres, NOT faceted gemstones, NOT bubbles. Transparent background, no ground, no pedestal, no text, no diagrams, no labels, no glow clouds or backdrop.
Cell order left to right top to bottom:
Row 1: (1) fluorine substituent: one small sage fluorine atom on a short silver attachment stub; (2) methyl group: one graphite carbon bonded tetrahedrally to three small pearl hydrogens; (3) hydroxyl group: a crimson oxygen bonded to a pearl hydrogen, with a silver attachment stub; (4) phenyl group: clear six-carbon graphite benzene ring with slim alternating double bonds, pearl hydrogen tips, a single short attachment stub.
Row 2: (5) morpholine: six-membered nonplanar chair ring of four graphite carbons one teal nitrogen one crimson oxygen, small hydrogens; (6) nitro group: teal nitrogen bonded to two crimson oxygens, Y-shaped compact geometry; (7) amide linker: graphite carbonyl carbon double bonded to crimson oxygen and bonded to teal nitrogen, two attachment stubs left and right; (8) sulfonamide: muted sulfur center with two crimson oxygen atoms and one teal nitrogen in tetrahedral arrangement, attachment stubs.
Row 3: (9) tert-butyl: compact branched cluster of four graphite carbons and small pearl hydrogens; (10) pyridine: six-member aromatic ring five graphite carbons one teal nitrogen, slim double bonds; (11) cyclopropyl: triangle of three graphite carbons and tiny pearl hydrogens, clearly triangular outline; (12) carboxylic acid: graphite carbon with one double bonded crimson oxygen and one crimson oxygen bonded to pearl hydrogen.
Row 4: (13) central scaffold: large clean six-carbon graphite benzene hexagon in same camera angle, open center, six short outward silver attachment stubs, NO instrument disk; (14) a single small graphite carbon atom; (15) a single small pearl hydrogen atom; (16) short two-carbon zigzag chain with silver bonds and small pearl hydrogens.
All objects look like one premium rendered molecular specimen kit, readable silhouettes, realistic reflections and occlusion. The atlas must have real alpha transparency around and between all sixteen objects.
